## 2. Automatic texting - rules as built (all per agency)

* **Off by default.** Nothing is queued unless the agency's texting is on and open-shift alerts are on. Settings, limits and sender are stored **per agency** (`sms_settings`); one agency's limits and sender number never affect another (tested).
* **Purposes** (each can be switched off): recruitment offer, call-out receipt (to the caller only), cancellation notice (assigned caregiver and requesters), confirmation (selected caregiver only), call-out and escalation alerts (on-call numbers of the coordinating office, else the agency-wide ones). The office is also told in-app/email per each manager's preference.
* **Recruitment window:** only for shifts that start within the agency's window, 14 days by default and never more than 14 (the same horizon as the caregiver board). Outside it nothing is queued; the scheduled run picks the shift up when it enters the window.
* **Quiet hours** (agency local time, 21:00-08:00 by default, any window incl. across midnight): caregiver texts are held until the window ends. Not held: call-out receipts, on-call alerts, and shifts starting within 12 hours. (Tested.)
* **24-hour escalation:** if a shift is still unfilled the configured hours (default 24, 1 to 72) after its first alert round, the office (in-app/email) and on-call numbers are alerted once per round. (Tested.)
* **Opt-in:** a text goes only to someone who entered their own number and ticked the explicit yes (in-app consent, version recorded). No consent, no text; consent is checked again when the message is claimed for sending.
* **STOP / START / HELP** (`system_sms_inbound`): STOP, STOPALL, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, END, QUIT opt the person out at once and cancel their queued texts; START/UNSTOP/YES re-subscribes **only** someone who had opted in before; HELP changes nothing (the carrier or provider answers HELP). A reply to one agency's sender number never changes another agency's contacts. **The inbound webhook is not exposed**: there is no public route; the function is called by the sender integration once one is chosen.
* **Duplicate prevention:** one text per person, shift, purpose and round (unique key per agency); repeated runs, retries and double taps cannot send twice.
* **Limits:** per agency per hour and per day, and per person per day. An over-limit text is recorded as skipped, not sent. Retry rule for failures: 5, 15, 45, 135 minutes, 5 attempts.
* **Content:** service city, date and time only. Never a client name, client code, street address or ZIP. Caregiver texts end "Reply STOP to opt out." Phone numbers are never written to logs or the audit trail (history shows the last four digits).

## 4. What sender setup needs (decision and work for the owner; nothing here is started)

Facts from AWS documentation (searched 2026-11-23; AWS pages change, re-check before ordering anything):
* US texting through AWS End User Messaging SMS needs an **origination identity** (a number); AWS describes 10DLC (brand and campaign registration, roughly 7 to 10 days for approval), toll-free (simpler, suited to low volume) and short codes (highest volume, slowest and costliest). A new account is in a **sandbox** (verified destination numbers only, up to 10) until a support case moves it to production.
* Each agency has its own sender settings here (name shown, sender number, limits). With 10DLC the brand and campaign are registered per business, so **each agency that wants its own number needs its own registration** and its own approval; decide whether agencies share one Ogevia number (simplest, but replies and opt-outs must then be routed by the agency's text label, which this build does not do) or each uses its own.
* Needed before any live text, in this order: (1) owner decision on provider and number type; (2) brand and campaign registration or toll-free verification for each sending agency, with the consent wording shown in the app and the STOP/HELP behavior; (3) production access for the AWS account; (4) a Business Associate review if any text could ever carry health information (the texts are designed to carry none); (5) the inbound reply path (provider webhook to `system_sms_inbound`), which needs a new route or queue and an approval; (6) cost model (per-message and monthly number fees) and an AWS Budget; (7) a live sender implementation behind the existing interface and a separate owner approval to switch an agency from `mock` to `live`.
* Open questions that change the design: one number per agency or shared; toll-free versus 10DLC; whether on-call numbers are personal phones (consent wording for staff) or agency phones.
